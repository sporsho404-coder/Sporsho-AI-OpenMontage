# Client Taste

What each client actually likes — learned from real deliveries, not from their brief.

A brief says what a client *thinks* they want. This file records what they *actually*
approve. When these disagree, this file is right.

---

## Why Separate From `CLIENTS/`

`CLIENTS/<client>/EDITING-RULES.md` holds the **stated** rules — what to apply.
This file holds the **observed** reality — what survived contact with the client.

Both matter. When they diverge, that divergence is the most useful thing you know
about that client.

## The Stated-vs-Observed Table Is The Point

Clients describe taste in the vocabulary they have, which is usually not editorial
vocabulary. Real examples of this pattern:

| They say | They often mean |
|---|---|
| "more energy" | clearer, more confident — *not* faster |
| "make it pop" | higher contrast, stronger colour — *not* more effects |
| "more professional" | fewer effects, cleaner typography |
| "more cinematic" | shallower depth of field, slower pacing, letterbox, score |
| "less busy" | fewer overlays — *not* fewer cuts |
| "punchier" | tighter cuts with clearer audio hits |

Fill this in from real revisions, not from theory. Every row should trace to a
specific revision round in that client's `REVISION-LOG.md`.
